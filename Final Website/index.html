About Me

Background

I’m a first-year at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC), class of 2030, majoring in Biology on the pre-med track. I’m also considering a Business in Health minor, because I want to understand how clinics are run as well as how patients are treated. I was born in the Philippines and lived there until I was six, when I moved to Raleigh, NC to join my parents. My dad was born and raised in the US, and my mom moved here shortly after I was born.

For three years I worked at Halo, a small regenerative health and wellness clinic where much of the work centered on peptide therapies. I started as an intern and worked as a medical assistant, mostly during the summers, often logging more than 30 hours a week. My clinical work included running labs, taking patient vitals, preparing injection mixtures, assisting with EKG prep and IV setups, and operating equipment such as the red light therapy device and the Visia skin analysis machine. On the administrative side, I handled prior authorizations, managed the physicians’ inboxes, and helped build and set up the office. I also trained a new employee to do the work I had been doing. Near the end, I used some basic coding to build an inventory management system for the clinic. I also listened in on conversations between doctors and patients, who ranged from young athletes to older adults hoping to regain their youthfulness, and it showed me that care has to fit what each person needs from their body day to day.

Fencing is the activity I’ve put the most into outside of school. I fenced epee for five years and earned my B rating. I qualified for and competed at the Junior Olympics and at the Division II Summer Nationals, where I placed 62nd. Competing at that level taught me to stay composed under pressure and to adjust to an opponent in the middle of a bout.

Future Goals

In the short term, I want to join a stem cell research lab to learn the mechanisms behind newer studies on regeneration and injury recovery. I also plan to study abroad, and I’m interested in Scotland. I want to see how another country structures its care and what changes when a public health system replaces a private one, including how access looks when the incentives are different.

Long term, I want to become a physician and open a clinic in the Philippines. I still have family there, and some of them are also going into medicine. I’ve seen how much distance there is between where they are and the kind of access they have, and I want to help close it.
